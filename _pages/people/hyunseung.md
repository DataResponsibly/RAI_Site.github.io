**Postdoctoral Associate**  

{% include figure.html people-profile=true path="assets/img/people/Hyunseung.jpg" title="Hyunseung" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" %}

Hyunseung (Daniel) Hwang is a postdoctoral associate at the NYU Center for Responsible AI, where he works with Julia Stoyanovich. He completed his Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering at [KAIST](https://www.kaist.ac.kr/en/), advised by Steven Euijong Whang. His dissertation introduced XClusters, a method that makes explainability part of the clustering objective. 

His current research examines how reliable SHAP and other feature-attribution methods are as tools for explaining and auditing models. He has shown that routine preprocessing choices, such as how age is bucketed or how race is encoded, can change which features SHAP ranks as most important, and that this can be exploited to make a discriminatory model look neutral.

In his work on explanation multiplicity, he found that SHAP can give different explanations from one run to the next, even for the same model, input, and prediction, and that small differences in attribution values can hide large changes in the top-ranked features. He has developed ways to measure this variation and to tell how much of it comes from model training and how much from the explainer itself.
